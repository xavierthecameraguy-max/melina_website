# Your website: how to make changes

**Milena's guide to editing www.onyxworldtravel.com in Google Sites**

Hi Milena!

This guide is yours to keep. It shows you, step by step, how to change your website yourself: the words, the photos, your trips, your reviews and your gallery. It also shows you how to read the messages people send you from your website.

You'll use two free Google programs, both in your own Google account:

- **Google Sites**, where your website lives. You change it by clicking and typing, a bit like writing a letter.
- **Google Forms**, which holds the three forms on your website and the messages people send through them.

Three things to know before you start:

- **Looking around can't break anything.** Your changes save by themselves as you go, but nobody else sees them until you click **Publish**.
- **There's a safety net.** Google Sites remembers every earlier version of your website, and you can go back to one (section 14).
- **It's all free.** Your website, your forms and your messages cost nothing at Google. The only bills are the ones you already pay GoDaddy for your web address and your email.

Each section below is one job. Find the job you want and follow the steps in order.

## A few words you'll see

- **Editor:** the screen in Google Sites where you make changes. Your page is in the middle, and a panel is on the right.
- **The panel on the right:** it has three tabs at the top: **Insert**, **Pages** and **Themes**. You'll use **Pages** a lot, **Insert** now and then, and **Themes** never.
- **More (⋮):** Google's button with three dots, one above the other. It opens a short list of extra choices. You'll see it next to pages, photos and forms.
- **Section:** one band of a page, running across the whole page. Each trip row, each review and each row of gallery photos is its own section. That's on purpose: to add one, you copy a section and type over the copy.
- **Background colors:** your sections come in three colors. In the editor they're called **Style 1**, **Style 2** and **Style 3**. In this guide they're called by their colors:
  - **Style 1 = Cream** (most of your website)
  - **Style 2 = Sunshine** (the yellow sections)
  - **Style 3 = Dark** (the dark band at the bottom of some pages, and the footer)
- **Text styles:** every line of words has a style: **Title**, **Heading**, **Subheading**, **Normal text** or **Small text**. The design decides what each one looks like.
- **Footer:** the dark band at the very bottom of every page. It's the same on every page.
- **Preview:** shows your changes the way visitors will see them, on a computer or on a phone. Nothing goes live.
- **Publish:** puts your changes on your real website, for everyone.
- **Hidden page:** a page that isn't in your menu. Your trip pages, your Trip template, Terms & conditions and Privacy are hidden pages. People reach them from buttons and links.
- **Form:** a box on your website where visitors type a message to you. Each message they send is called a **response** in Google Forms.
- **Tab:** a page open in your internet program (Chrome, Safari or Edge). The tabs sit side by side at the very top of the window. Some links open in a new tab.

## 1. Open your website editor

1. On your computer, open your internet program (Chrome, Safari or Edge) and go to **sites.google.com**.
2. If Google asks you to sign in, sign in with your Google account ([TO FILL: Google account email for Milena]) and your password.
3. Under your recent sites, click **Onyx World Travel**.

You're in! Your website is in the middle of the screen, and the panel is on the right.

**To go to another page:** in the panel on the right, click **Pages**, then click the page's name. Some pages sit inside another page: your trip pages and your Trip template are inside **Services & Trips**. If you don't see them, click the small arrow next to **Services & Trips** to open it.

Good to know:

- **Use a computer** for every job in this guide. Google Sites is made for editing on a computer.
- **There's no Save button.** Your changes save by themselves.
- **If you get lost, close the tab.** Your work is saved, and nothing is live until you publish.
- **Only ever open "Onyx World Travel".** If you see a site called "Onyx World Travel SPARE, do not edit", that's a spare copy Xavier made. Leave it alone. And never click **Blank site** or one of the ready-made designs on the sites.google.com start page: that would start a brand-new, separate website.


## 2. Change any words

1. Open your editor (section 1) and go to the page.
2. Click into the words you want to change. A small bar of buttons appears above them.
3. Type your new words. To delete words, use the Backspace key.
   - **Bold:** highlight the words and click **B** in the small bar.
   - **A new line in the same style:** click at the end of a line and press Enter.
4. **Words on a button:** click the button once, then click **Edit** (the pencil). Change the words in the **Name** box. Leave the link under it alone, unless this guide tells you to change it. Click **Done**.
5. **Words that are a link** (they look underlined or colored, like "Send me a message"): you can retype the words, and the link stays.
6. Check your work (section 4), then **Publish** (section 5).

**Never change the font, the size or the color** of any words. The design takes care of all of that, and a box you change by hand stops following the design. The **B** button is fine. So is the style list (Title, Heading, Subheading, Normal text, Small text) when this guide tells you which one to pick.

**Square brackets.** If you see something like "[TO FILL: how fast Milena replies, e.g. within a day]", that's a spot Xavier left for you, because only you know the answer. Click it, delete everything from the [ to the ], and type the real words. If you don't have the answer yet, leave it, but don't publish a page where visitors would see it. Section 4 shows you how to find them all.

**Pasting words** from an email or a document can bring in a different look. To paste plain words that match the rest of the page, hold down **Ctrl** and **Shift** and press **V** (on a Mac, **Command**, **Shift** and **V**). Or simply type them.

**The footer** (the dark band at the very bottom) is the same on every page. Change it once, on any page, and it changes everywhere. **Never delete anything in the footer to "fix" one page**: it disappears from every page.

**Once a year, in January:** look at the footer. If the year after the © is last year, click it, type the new year, and **Publish**.


## 3. Swap a photo

1. Open your editor and go to the page.
2. Click the photo once. A small bar of buttons appears above it.
3. Click **More** (⋮), then **Replace image**. Click **Upload** and pick the new photo on your computer.

   If you don't see **Replace image**: click the photo, press the **Delete** key, then in the panel on the right click **Insert**, then **Images**, then **Upload**, and pick the new photo. Drag it into the old photo's place.
4. **Describe the photo:** click the photo, click **More** (⋮), then **Add alt text**. Type one short sentence about what's in it, for example: "Four friends smiling at the rim of a red-rock canyon". People who can't see the screen hear this read out loud, and Google reads it too.
5. **If the photo looks too tall or too wide:** click it, click **Crop**, and drag the edges until it looks like the photos around it. Click outside the photo when you're done.
6. Look in **Preview** (section 4) to make sure nothing important is cut off, like someone's head.
7. **Publish** (section 5).

Good to know:

- **JPG, PNG and GIF photos work.** Some iPhone photos are another kind (HEIC) and won't upload. Try emailing the photo to yourself from your phone, then save it from the email and upload that copy. Or, on your iPhone, go to **Settings**, then **Camera**, then **Formats**, and choose **Most Compatible**: new photos will then upload fine.
- **Photos straight from your phone are fine.** You don't need to make them smaller first.
- **A few pictures are special drawings Xavier made:** the postcard at the top of your Home page, the 1-2-3 numbers and the little round icons. Leave those as they are.
- **Only post photos of travelers who've said it's OK.**


## 4. Check before you publish

A two-minute check saves headaches. Before every **Publish**:

1. At the top of the editor, click **Preview** (the eye).
2. At the bottom right, choose the phone view, then the computer view. Many of your travelers will visit on their phones, so always look at the phone view.
3. Read what you changed: names, dates, prices, spelling.
4. **Find any square brackets you forgot.** Hold down **Ctrl** and press **F** (on a Mac, **Command** and **F**). A small search box opens. Type **TO FILL** and press Enter. If it finds something on a page visitors will see, fill it in or delete it. Do this on each page you changed. (Your **Trip template** is full of them on purpose: they show you what to fill in on each new trip. Leave the template as it is.)
5. **Click every button and link you added or changed.** Each one must do something real:
   - open the right page, or
   - open your booking form, or
   - start an email to you.

   If one doesn't, fix it or delete it. A button that goes nowhere makes people think your website is broken.
6. At the bottom right, click **Exit** to go back to the editor.

If you changed a form or added a trip, also send yourself a test after you publish (section 13, part E).


## 5. Publish your changes

1. At the top right of the editor, click **Publish**.
2. If a window opens showing what you changed, look it over and click **Publish** in that window.
3. Wait a moment, then open www.onyxworldtravel.com to see your change.

Good to know:

- **Every change in Google Sites needs a new Publish.** Until then, only you can see it, in the editor.
- **Changes in Google Forms are different.** They save by themselves, and the forms on your website should use them right away. If Google Forms ever shows a **Publish** or **Update** button at the top right after you change a form, click it.
- **Don't see your change yet?** Wait a few minutes and refresh the page, or look on your phone. You can also open a private window: in Chrome or Edge, hold down **Ctrl** and **Shift** and press **N** (on a Mac, **Command**, **Shift** and **N**). Then go to www.onyxworldtravel.com in that window.
- **Never touch the small arrow next to Publish** (Publish settings). It holds your web address and whether Google can find your website. Xavier set both up, and they should stay as they are.

## 6. Add a new trip

This is the biggest job in this guide, so pick a quiet time and go slowly. You'll do six things: copy your Trip template, fill it in, add the trip to your booking form, add the trip's row on two pages, then check and publish.

**Before you start**

- Give yourself a safe point to come back to (section 14, part A). Name it something like "Before Greece 2027".
- Have the trip's details ready:
  - the trip's name, the place and the dates
  - how many days
  - the price per person sharing a room, and the price for a room to yourself
  - the deposit, and when the rest is due
  - the group size, and how many spots there are
  - the activity level, and about how much walking there is each day
  - the book-by date
  - the day-by-day plan
  - what's included, and what isn't
  - your before-you-go notes (passport, visa, travel insurance)
  - a couple of questions people ask, with your answers
  - a nice photo, wider than it is tall

### Part A: Copy your Trip template

1. Open your editor (section 1).
2. In the panel on the right, click **Pages**. Click the small arrow next to **Services & Trips** if you need to, and find **Trip template**.
3. Point at **Trip template** with your mouse and click its **More** (⋮).
4. Click **Duplicate page**. A copy appears in the list, probably called "Copy of Trip template".
5. Click the copy's **More** (⋮), then **Properties**.
   1. In the name box, type the trip's short name, for example **Greece 2027**. Keep it short: the name becomes part of the trip's web address.
   2. If you see **Advanced**, click it. If there's a **Custom path** box, type the same name in small letters with dashes instead of spaces, for example **greece-2027**.
   3. Click **Done**.
6. **Make sure the new page is hidden.** A hidden page has a small "hidden" mark next to its name in the list, the same mark Trip template has. If yours doesn't: click its **More** (⋮), then **Hide from navigation**. Trip pages stay out of your menu. People find them from the trip rows.
7. **Where it sits:** the new page should be inside **Services & Trips**, next to Trip template. If it landed somewhere else, you can drag it onto **Services & Trips** to tidy up. It works wherever it is, as long as it's hidden.

Good to know:

- **Name the page once, now, and don't rename it later.** Renaming a page changes its web address, so any link you've already sent people would stop working. If a trip's name changes later, just change the words on the page.
- **Never fill in or delete Trip template itself.** It's your master copy for every future trip.

### Part B: Fill in the new trip page

1. In **Pages**, click your new page (for example "Greece 2027") to open it. The page you're on is highlighted in the list. Make sure it's the new page, not Trip template.
2. Work down the page from top to bottom, and replace every [TO FILL: ...] with the real details:
   - **Top of the page:** the trip's name, and the one-line teaser under the labels. In the small bold labels line, delete " · Sample trip", so it reads "Spots open · Milena is coming on this trip". Swap the photo (section 3) and describe it.
   - **Quick facts:** every line.
   - **Why I picked this trip:** two or three lines in your own words.
   - **Day by day:** each day is two lines: a "Day 1: ..." line and a line about what happens. To add a day, highlight the last day's two lines, copy them (**Ctrl** and **C**; on a Mac, **Command** and **C**), click at the very end of them, press Enter, and paste (**Ctrl** and **V**; on a Mac, **Command** and **V**). Then type over the copy. That way the new day looks just like the others. To remove a day, highlight its two lines and press Backspace.
   - **What's included / Not included:** one item per line. Always keep "Milena, along for the whole trip".
   - **Before you go.**
   - **Questions about this trip:** each question is a fold-out box. Click the question to change it, and click the answer under it to change that.
     - **To add a question:** in the panel on the right, click **Insert**, then **Collapsible text**. If it lands at the bottom of the page, drag it up under your last question. Type the question in its top box and the answer in the box under it.
     - **To remove a question:** click its box, then click **Delete group**.
   - **Save my spot** (the last section): change the line "Trip: ..." to the trip's name and dates.
3. **Leave these as they are:**
   - the small "All upcoming trips" link at the top
   - both **Save my spot** buttons. They open your booking form, the same form for every trip.
   - the line "In the form, choose this trip under “Which trip?”."
   - the form itself, and the phone lines around it.

### Part C: Add the trip to your booking form

Your trip pages all share one booking form. Its first question, "Which trip?", is a list of your trips. Add the new one:

1. Go to **forms.google.com** and click **Trip booking and waitlist**.
2. Click the question **Which trip?**.
3. Click **Add option**. Type the trip's name, a comma and its dates, exactly as on the trip page. For example: **Greece 2027, June 1 to 10, 2027**
4. **"A different trip, or I'm not sure yet" always stays last.** If your new trip landed under it, point at your new trip's line, and drag it up by the little dots at its left.

   If dragging is fiddly, there's another way: click into the "A different trip, or I'm not sure yet" line and type your new trip over it. Then click **Add option** and type **A different trip, or I'm not sure yet** again. It's now last.
5. That's it. Google Forms saves by itself.

### Part D: Add the trip's row on your Home page

1. Back in the editor (sites.google.com), go to your **Home** page and find "Where are we going next?". Under it, each trip has its own row: a photo on the left, the trip's details on the right.
2. Point at a trip's row with your mouse, without clicking. A few small buttons appear on the left edge of the row. Click **Duplicate section**. A copy appears right under it.

   The two rows are now the same, so it doesn't matter which one you change. Trips go soonest first: if the new trip comes **before** the old one, change the **upper** row. If it comes **after**, change the **lower** row.

   If the copy lands at the very bottom of the page instead, drag it up by the handle on its left edge until it sits with the other trip rows.
3. In the row you're changing, change:
   - the photo (section 3)
   - the trip's name
   - the place and the dates
   - the price per person

   The small bold label should say **Spots open**. (If you copied a sold-out trip's row, change it back to Spots open.)
4. **Point the button at the new trip page.** Click the **See the trip** button once, then **Edit** (the pencil). In the link box, delete what's there, start typing the trip's page name (for example "Greece"), and pick your new page from the list. Click **Done**.

   Careful: a copied row still goes to the old trip until you do this. And always pick the page from the list. Never paste a web address into the link box, because a pasted address opens in a new tab.
5. "More trips are on the way" always stays under your trip rows.
6. **If this is your first trip after a time with none:** the words under "Where are we going next?" say "I get asked this a lot. The next one is in the works." Change them back to **I get asked this a lot. Here's the answer.**

**If there's no trip row left to copy** (because you took them all down), build a new one:

1. In the panel on the right, click **Insert**. Scroll down to **Layouts**, and click the one with a picture on the left and words on the right. It appears at the bottom of the page.
2. Click the picture box (the **+**) and upload the trip's photo. Add its alt text (section 3).
3. Click the words box next to it and type, one line each:
   - the trip's name. Choose **Subheading** for it from the style list in the small bar.
   - **Spots open**. Choose **Small text**, and make it bold with **B**.
   - the place, a space, a dot (·), a space and the dates, for example: Greece · June 1 to 10, 2027. Choose **Normal text**.
   - the price, for example: $2,950 per person. Choose **Normal text**.
4. In the panel on the right, click **Insert**, then **Button**. Name: **See the trip**. In the link box, pick your new trip page from the list. Click **Insert**. Drag the button under the words. Click the button, click its small down arrow, and choose **Outlined**.
5. Point at the new row, click **Background** on its left edge, and choose **Style 1** (Cream).
6. Drag the row up by the handle on its left edge until it sits between "Where are we going next?" and "More trips are on the way".
7. If any empty box with a **+** is left in the row, click it and delete it. Empty boxes look broken to visitors.
8. Then do step 6 above: change "The next one is in the works." back to **Here's the answer.**

### Part E: Add the same row on your Services & Trips page

1. Go to your **Services & Trips** page and find "Upcoming Trips".
2. Do steps 2 to 5 of Part D again. Here, "Don't see what you want?" always stays under your trip rows.
3. If there's no trip row here to copy, build one the same way as in Part D ("If there's no trip row left to copy"), and drag it between "Upcoming Trips" and "Don't see what you want?".
4. **If this is your first trip after a time with none:** the words under "Upcoming Trips" say "Where are we going next? The next trip is in the works." Change them back to **Where are we going next? Here's what's coming up.**

### Part F: Check, publish and test

1. Do the check in section 4. In **Preview**:
   - On Home and on Services & Trips, click the new row's **See the trip**. Both must open the new trip page.
   - On the new trip page, click both **Save my spot** buttons. Each should open your booking form in a new tab, with your new trip in the "Which trip?" list.
2. **Publish** (section 5).
3. Send yourself a test from the new trip page, choosing the new trip (section 13, part E).


## 7. Change dates, price or spots left

**Spots left**

1. Open your editor and go to the trip's page (**Pages**, then the trip's name).
2. In "Quick facts", change the number after "Spots left:".
3. **Publish**.

If the trip is now full, do section 8 instead.

**A trip's dates or price changed**

These show up in more than one place, so change every one:

1. **On the trip's page:** "Quick facts" (the dates, the prices, the deposit and the book-by date), and the "Trip: ..." line in the last section, just above the form.
2. **On your Home page** ("Where are we going next?") **and your Services & Trips page** ("Upcoming Trips"): the dates and the price in this trip's row.
3. **In your booking form:** go to forms.google.com > **Trip booking and waitlist** > click **Which trip?** > click into this trip's line and change the dates. Messages you've already received keep the old wording. That's fine.
4. Check (section 4) and **Publish** (section 5).


## 8. Mark a trip sold out

When a trip is full, its page stays up, and the booking form takes waitlist requests instead. You only change words. Nothing gets deleted.

**On the trip's page:**

1. **The small bold labels line at the top:** change "Spots open" to **Sold out: join the waitlist**. Leave the rest of the line as it is.
2. **Quick facts:** change the "Spots left" line to **Spots left: Sold out (waitlist open)**.
3. **Both Save my spot buttons** (one at the top, one under Quick facts): click each one, click **Edit** (the pencil), and change the Name to **Join the waitlist**. Don't change the link under it: it's the same form. Click **Done**.
4. **The last section:** change its heading "Save my spot" to **Join the waitlist**.
5. **Under the form:** change "No payment yet. I'll be in touch to confirm your spot and explain payment." to **No payment. I'll let you know as soon as a spot opens up.**

**On your Home page ("Where are we going next?") and your Services & Trips page ("Upcoming Trips"):**

6. In this trip's row, change "Spots open" to **Sold out: join the waitlist**.

**In your booking form:**

7. Go to forms.google.com > **Trip booking and waitlist** > click **Which trip?** > click into this trip's line, and add **(sold out: join the waitlist)** to the end of it. For example:

   Greece 2027, June 1 to 10, 2027 (sold out: join the waitlist)

   Now, when someone picks this trip, you'll know right away it's a waitlist request. The message people see after they press Submit already covers the waitlist, so you don't need to change it.

**Then:**

8. Check (section 4) and **Publish** (section 5).
9. Send yourself a test from the trip's page (section 13, part E), choosing the sold-out trip.

**If a spot opens up again,** change the words back:

- "Spots open": the labels line at the top, and in both trip rows.
- "Spots left: 1" (or however many there are).
- "Save my spot": both buttons, and the heading of the last section.
- Under the form: "No payment yet. I'll be in touch to confirm your spot and explain payment."
- In the booking form, take "(sold out: join the waitlist)" off the end of the trip's line.
- Then **Publish**.


## 9. Take down a finished trip

When a trip is over, take it off your lists of upcoming trips, and turn its page into a friendly "wrapped up" page for anyone who still has the link.

Before you start, give yourself a safe point (section 14, part A).

### Part A: Remove the trip's rows

1. Go to your **Home** page and find "Where are we going next?".
2. Point at this trip's row, without clicking. On its left edge, click **Delete section**. Delete only this trip's row. "More trips are on the way" always stays.
3. Go to your **Services & Trips** page, find "Upcoming Trips", and do the same. "Don't see what you want?" always stays.
4. **Was that your last upcoming trip?** Then change two sentences, so your pages don't promise trips that aren't there:
   - **Home**, under "Where are we going next?": change "I get asked this a lot. Here's the answer." to **I get asked this a lot. The next one is in the works.**
   - **Services & Trips**, under "Upcoming Trips": change "Where are we going next? Here's what's coming up." to **Where are we going next? The next trip is in the works.**

   When you add your next trip, section 6 tells you to change them back.

### Part B: Turn the trip page into a "wrapped up" page

1. Go to the trip's page (**Pages**, then the trip's name).
2. **The small bold labels line at the top:** change "Spots open" (or "Sold out: join the waitlist") to **This trip has wrapped up**.
3. **The button at the top:** click it, click **Edit** (the pencil), and:
   - change the Name to **See my upcoming trips**
   - in the link box, delete the form's link, and pick your **Services & Trips** page from the list
   - click **Done**.
4. **Quick facts:** delete the "Spots left" and "Book by" lines. Then click the **Save my spot** button under the list and press the **Delete** key (or click **Remove**).
5. **The last section:**
   1. Change its heading to **This one has wrapped up**.
   2. Change the line "Booking for Mom or Dad? Call me at ..." to: **But I'm always planning the next adventure. Come see where we're going next.**
   3. Delete the "Trip: ..." line and the "In the form, choose this trip ..." line.
   4. Click the edge of the form box and press the **Delete** key.
   5. Delete the two lines that were under the form ("Rather talk? ..." and "No payment ...").
   6. Add a button: in the panel on the right, click **Insert**, then **Button**. Name: **See upcoming trips**. In the link box, pick **Services & Trips** from the list. Click **Insert**. Drag the button under your words.
   7. If you like, point at the section, click **Background** on its left edge, and choose **Style 3** (Dark).

### Part C: Remove the trip from your booking form

1. Go to forms.google.com > **Trip booking and waitlist** > click **Which trip?**.
2. Point at this trip's line and click the **X** at its right.

The messages you've already received keep the trip's name. Nothing is lost.

### Part D: Check and publish

1. Check (section 4). On Home and Services & Trips, the trip's rows are gone. On the trip's page, the top button goes to Services & Trips.
2. **Publish** (section 5).

The trip's page stays hidden, so it's not in your menu or your trip lists. Anyone who saved its link sees your "wrapped up" note.

**In a hurry?** You can do Parts A and C, then delete the trip's page instead of Part B: in **Pages**, click the trip page's **More** (⋮), then **Delete**, then **Publish**. The only difference: anyone with the old link sees Google's "page not found" message instead of your friendly note.

**Never delete Trip template.**

## 10. Add a review

Your reviews are on the Reviews page, one section per review. To add one, you copy a review section and change the words.

Before you start, ask the traveler if it's OK to put their words on your website, and how they'd like their name to appear (for example "Kellie G., Greece").

1. Open your editor and go to the **Reviews** page.
2. Point at the last review (Haley E.'s), without clicking. On its left edge, click **Duplicate section**. A copy appears right under it.

Your website has three forms. Each one keeps every message in Google Forms, in your Google account, and emails you when a new one arrives.

### Part A: Where messages arrive

## 14. Undo a mistake

**Just made a mistake?** At the top of the editor, click **Undo** (the arrow curving to the left), or hold down **Ctrl** and press **Z** (on a Mac, **Command** and **Z**). Click it again to undo more.

If you haven't published yet, nobody has seen the mistake.

### Part A: Name a safe point (before big jobs)

Do this before you add a trip, take one down, or delete anything.

1. Open your editor.
2. At the top right, click **More** (⋮), then **Version history**.
3. On the right, the newest version is at the top of the list. Click its **More** (⋮), then **Rename**.
4. Type a name you'll recognize, like "Before Greece 2027", and click **Save**.
5. At the top left, click **Back** to return to your editor.

There's already one called **Launched**. Xavier made it the day your website went live.

### Part B: Go back to an earlier version

1. Open your editor. At the top right, click **More** (⋮), then **Version history**.
2. On the right, click **Expand detailed versions** to see every change, with the date and time.
3. Click a version from before the mistake (or one of your named safe points). Your website shows how it looked then.
4. Now choose:
   - **To bring back only the page you're looking at** (for example, one trip page, or a page you deleted by accident): at the top left, click **Restore this page version**, then **Restore page**.
   - **To bring back the whole website** as it was then: at the top left, click **Restore this site version**, then **Restore**.
5. Check in **Preview** that it looks right.
6. **Publish** (section 5).

**Changed your mind?** Before you click Restore, you can click **Back** at the top left, and nothing changes.

Please know: going back undoes **everything** changed after that version, on that page (or on the whole website), not only the mistake.

**Version history doesn't cover your forms.** In Google Forms, undo a mistake straight away: hold down **Ctrl** and press **Z** (on a Mac, **Command** and **Z**), or click **Undo** if Google offers it. A deleted message can't be brought back.


## 15. What you can ignore

Google shows lots of offers, helpers and extras. You don't need any of them.

- **Gemini.** Google sometimes offers its automatic writing helper. It may be called Gemini, show a sparkle icon (✦), or say "Help me create" or "Help me write". You don't need it, and nothing on your website needs it. Just close it. Your own words are what your travelers love.
- **Themes** (the third tab in the panel on the right). Never pick another theme or change the colors or fonts. One click can change the look of your whole website.
- **The font, size and color buttons** in the small bar above your words.
- **Insert > Embed.** Never use it.
- **Settings** (the gear at the top). Everything in there was set up by Xavier and should stay as it is.
- **The arrow next to Publish** (Publish settings). It holds your web address.
- **The Share button.** It decides who can see and change your website. Leave it as it is.
- **"Try Google Workspace", "Get a domain", and offers of more storage.** Your free account already has everything your website needs.
- **Starting a new site.** On the sites.google.com start page, never click **Blank site** or a ready-made design. That would be a brand-new, separate website.
- **In Google Forms:** **Add-ons**, **Make this a quiz**, and new kinds of questions such as **File upload**. Some of these would make visitors sign in to Google before they can write to you, and many would give up.
- **On your live website:** the magnifying glass at the top right (a search box Google adds) and the small round **(i)** at the bottom left. Every Google website has them. You didn't break anything, and there's nothing you need to do about them.
